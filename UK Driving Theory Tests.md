## Some Important Numbers
> [!NOTE] National Speed Limits
> - $70$mph for dual carriageway and motorway
> - $60$mph for single carriageway
> - $30$mph for built-up area, indicated by street lights(20mph in Wales)
> - When towing a trailer or caravan, the speed limit is lowered by 10mph on carriageway and motorway

> [!NOTE] Distance to the car in front
> - On a normal road in good dry condition
> 	- 2 seconds time gap
> - On a wet road
> 	- Should be doubled, at least 4 seconds
> - On an icy road
> 	- Should be ten times longer than dry condition, at least 20 seconds

## Horns and Lights

> [!faq] When are we not allowed to use our horns?
> - Do not use your horn between 11:30 p.m. and 7:00 a.m. in built-up areas
> - It is illegal to use your horn when you are stationary in traffic
> - Don't use the horn to express annoyance or get attention

## Driving Techniques

> [!faq] When can you drive without seat belt
> - Reversing
> - Medically exempt, but must carry certificate(pregnant)

- Tyre pressures should be checked when tyres are cold
- Engine oil level must be checked before a long journey

> [!NOTE] Kick-down
> "kick down" is a function in an automatic transmission that occurs when you press the accelerator pedal all the way to the floor, forcing the car to downshift to a lower gear for a sudden increase in power. This provides extra acceleration for situations like overtaking or going up a steep hill

> [!NOTE] ABS
> - ABS might be ineffective on loose surface

> [!faq] What would suggest you are driving on icy road
> - There is less tyre noise
> - Steering feels light and unresponsive

### Gear Selection
- Miss out some gears can actually save fuel
- High gear produces less torque, but better fuel efficiency
	- Low torque is desirable on icy surface as it reduces the chance of wheelspin
- Low gear produces more torque, and can slow the car down using engine braking when going downhill
### Trailer Towing
- Take professional training before towing a trailer for the first time
- When loading a caravan/trailer, puts heavy items over the axle to reduce risk of swerving
- Your are allowed to tow a trailer up to 3,500 kg MAM (maximum authorized mass, trailer + good) without additional test
- Breakaway cable must be fitted to the trailer braking system
- A stabilizer fitted to the tow bar can improve the handling when towing

> [!warning]
> - You should not use the right lane when towing on three or more lanes  motorway, unless there are exceptional circumstances such as lane closure

### Parking

> [!NOTE] Red Routes
> Red routes are ==major roads in urban areas where stopping is strictly prohibited to keep traffic flowing==. **Double red lines** mean no stopping at any time, while **single red lines** mean no stopping during the times shown on nearby signs. Parking and loading are only allowed in designated bays.

- Single yellow line means no waiting/parking at specific times
- Double yellow times means no waiting/parking at any times, but loading/unloading passengers/goods are allowed
- Double white lines(even one of them is broken) also means no parking other than pick up and set down passengers
## Emergency 

> [!NOTE] Re-joining from emergency area
> - MUST use the emergency telephone and follow the operator's instruction
> - A lane may need to be closed for safe rejoining

- The warning triangle should be placed 45 meters from your vehicle

> [!warning] 
> DO NOT use warning triangle on motorways 

### Using the Emergency Telephone
- To find the nearest emergency telephone, **follow the direction shown on the marker posts**
- **Face oncoming traffic** when using the emergency telephone on the motorway
- After the call, wait for help to arrive **well away from the carriageway**

## Interaction with Authorities

### MOT
- **Validity and timing:**
    - How long is an MOT certificate normally valid for? 
	    - 1 YEAR
    - When does a new car need its first MOT? 
	    - 3 YEAR
- **Driving without a valid MOT:**
    - When is it legal to drive a vehicle that requires an MOT but doesn't have one? 
	    - Only when driving to a pre-arranged test appointment or to a garage for necessary repairs
    - What could be invalidated if you drive without a current MOT certificate? 
	    - Your insurance
- **Responsibilities:**
    - What is the owner's responsibility regarding the MOT? 
	    - They must ensure the vehicle has a valid MOT certificate before driving it on public roads).
### DVLA
- You must inform the licensing authority if poor health affects your driving

> [!faq] When must you update the registration certificate
> - when your personal details change (like your name or address)
> - when you sell or transfer ownership of the vehicle
> - when you make significant modifications to the vehicle

> [!NOTE] SORN
> SORN is a legal declaration in the UK that a vehicle is not being used or kept on a public road. It allows you to stop taxing and insuring the vehicle, but it also means you cannot legally drive it on any public road, including parking it there, even on a temporary basis
- SORN is valid until the vehicle is re-taxed, sold or scrapped
### Police
- You have seven days to produce a document at the station if you do not have it during a police stop
- Traffic police DO NOT have enforcement power(arresting etc.), but can stop and direct people on motorways and some 'A' class roads 
## Insurance

> [!NOTE] Third Party Insurance
> Third-party insurance is the ***minimum legal requirement*** for vehicle insurance in the UK, covering damage to other people's property or injuries to other people in an accident you cause. It does not cover damage to your own vehicle or your own medical expenses.

- A newly qualified driver must have valid motor insurance
- You must have valid insurance when applying to renew the vehicle tax 

> [!faq] When must you present valid insurance?
> 1. When the police stop you
> 2. When you are in a accident
## First Aid
### CPR
- During CPR, you should press down and release the centre of their chest 120 times per minute

- For Adult: Press down 5-6cm on the casualty's chest
- For Child: Press with only one hand and 4-5cm
- For Infants: Use only 2 fingers, 1/3 of their chest depth

### Burn treatment
- Douse the burns with clean, cool water
- DO NOT burst any blisters or remove anything sticking to the burns
